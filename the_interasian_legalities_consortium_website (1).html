About the Consortium

"Above all, we are a scholarly community rather than an institutional hub; your intellectual engagement is central to who we are and how we act."

Launched in April 2024, the InterAsian Legalities Consortium investigates contemporary realities and historical foundations underpinning Asian legal systems and cultures. It approaches Asia as a set of interconnected processes: as borders are being crossed more easily than ever before, abetted by technological advancements in digital infrastructure. Yet local legal regimes and actors continue to maintain pivotal socio-political and territorial identities.

This research partnership aims to undertake research, and develop educational initiatives, that are simultaneously cognisant of deeply local, and grounded, legal and governance realities while being aware and able to interpret global governance systems.

Interdisciplinary Synergy

Our network unites scholars from law, architecture, social sciences, humanities, and medicine. Through interdisciplinary collaborations, we foster research excellence and spearhead cooperative international grant applications.

Core Academic Pillars

Research Themes

Our collaborative inquiry is structured into four pivotal thematic teams led by prominent researchers across regional corridors.

Theme Lead

1. Global Faith

Led by Dr Maria Bhatti

Investigating how religion shapes law, finance, and society across Asia and beyond. Examining Islamic, Hindu, Buddhist, and other faith traditions in interaction with state law and global markets.

Focus Areas:

  • Religion, law, and legal pluralism
  • Faith-based and ethical finance
  • Digital finance and religious norms
  • Gender and economic inclusion
  • Religious charity & development (waqf, zakat, dana)
Theme Lead

2. Actors in Governance

Led by Dr Rangika Palliyaarachchi

Asking "Who really governs?" Beyond formal governments, studying courts, NGOs, corporations, community leaders, and international bodies sharing and competing for power in Asia.

Focus Areas:

  • Corporate power and private governance
  • Customary law and Indigenous governance
  • Digital governance dynamics
  • Mapping non-state regulatory orders
Theme Lead

3. Constitutional Connections

Led by Dr Rehan Abeyratne

Asian constitutions are inextricably linked by shared colonial histories and global doctrines. We examine how these cross-border connections shape constitutional law in practice, not just on paper.

Focus Areas:

  • How global legal norms travel and adapt
  • InterAsian constitutional histories
  • Legal pluralism and structural challenges
Theme Lead

4. Asian Fabrics of Governance

Led by Dr Jeremy Kingsley

Approaching Asia as a web of connected legal systems linked by trade routes, infrastructure, and the movement of people, money, and ideas across two key corridors:

Maritime Corridor: Indian & Pacific Oceans (Dubai, Singapore, Jakarta, Hong Kong, Tokyo)
Inland Corridor: Eurasian Land Bridge (Beirut, Astana, Islamabad, Beijing)

Core Projects:

  • Mapping connections & geopolitical frameworks
  • Infrastructures (social, technological, physical)
  • Disruptions to the global legal order

Basic Structure: Uncovering the InterAsian Colonial Roots of a Global Doctrine

Presented by: Prof. Rehan Abeyratne | Chaired by: Dr Meda Couzens

Re-evaluates the basic structure doctrine—which allows courts to strike down constitutional amendments threatening core principles like judicial independence.
